Chew a plain cracker for a minute and it starts to taste a little sweet — even though there's almost no sugar in it. The reason is an enzyme in your saliva, breaking in about a minute links that would otherwise last around a million years. In this video: what enzymes are, the active site and the lock-and-key model (and why it's a simplification), how temperature and pH affect enzymes, what 'denatured' really means — and two myths that cost marks in exams.
