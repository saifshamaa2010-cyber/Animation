| 6.1.5 | Core | Subsequent use and storage of carbohydrates: (a) starch as an energy store (b) cellulose for cell walls (c) glucose in respiration (d) sucrose for transport in the phloem (e) nectar to attract insects for pollination | S07 | All five, numbered, plus why starch is stored (insoluble → no effect on osmosis) |
| 6.1.6 | Core | Importance of (a) nitrate ions for making amino acids (b) magnesium ions for making chlorophyll | S08 | Both, with deficiency symptoms and the exam "ion → makes → without it" chain |
| 6.1.7 | Core | Investigate the need for chlorophyll, light and carbon dioxide, using appropriate controls | S11, S12 | Destarching, full starch test with the reason for each step, controls defined and named in every experiment (green parts; uncovered part; flask without soda lime); variegated leaf, black paper, soda lime with the leaf still attached to the destarched plant |

| 6.1.9 | Supplement | Investigate and describe the effect of light and dark conditions on gas exchange in an aquatic plant using hydrogencarbonate indicator solution | S16, S17 | Indicator colours; tubes sealed with bungs (why); pondweed (an aquatic plant) in light / in a foil-wrapped tube / no-pondweed control; dim light → no net uptake or release; net defined; respiration myth; the one pause-and-predict |

| 6.2.1 | Core | Most leaves have a large surface area and are thin; explain how these are adaptations for photosynthesis | S09 | Broad → absorb more light; thin → short diffusion distance for CO₂ |
