# EP002 — Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 (2026–2028) Topic 6 coverage map

Every learning statement in Topic 6 "Plant nutrition", Core and Supplement, and where the episode teaches it.
Statement wording checked 14 Oct 2026 against search extracts of the official 2026–2028 syllabus
([PDF][syl]; the Dec 2025 and Aug 2026 updates changed only a weblink and the assessment overview: [update][upd]),
cross-checked with copies of the syllabus ([SlideShare][ss], [Scribd][scribd]) and Save My Exams' CIE IGCSE notes.
The build machine cannot open cambridgeinternational.org directly, so wording comes from search extracts — spot-check
against the PDF if anything looks off. **Result: all 14 statements covered.**

| Ref | Tier | Syllabus statement (abridged) | Where | How |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1.1 | Core | Describe photosynthesis as the process by which plants synthesise carbohydrates from raw materials using energy from light | S03 | Definition said word for word, then broken down: synthesise = make, carbohydrate = glucose, raw materials, energy from light |
| 6.1.2 | Core | Word equation: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, in the presence of light and chlorophyll | S04 | Built term by term; "in the presence of light and chlorophyll" said verbatim; conditions on the arrow, not reactants; where CO₂ and water come from, where O₂ goes |
| 6.1.3 | Core | Chlorophyll is a green pigment found in chloroplasts | S06 | Lens into palisade cells; label "chlorophyll · green pigment" |
| 6.1.4 | Core | Chlorophyll transfers energy from light into energy in chemicals, for the synthesis of carbohydrates | S06 | Said verbatim; exam tip "transfers", not "makes" energy |
| 6.1.5 | Core | Subsequent use and storage of carbohydrates: (a) starch as an energy store (b) cellulose for cell walls (c) glucose in respiration (d) sucrose for transport in the phloem (e) nectar to attract insects for pollination | S07 | All five, numbered, plus why starch is stored (insoluble → no effect on osmosis) |
| 6.1.6 | Core | Importance of (a) nitrate ions for making amino acids (b) magnesium ions for making chlorophyll | S08 | Both, with deficiency symptoms and the exam "ion → makes → without it" chain |
| 6.1.7 | Core | Investigate the need for chlorophyll, light and carbon dioxide, using appropriate controls | S11, S12 | Destarching, full starch test with the reason for each step, controls defined; variegated leaf, black paper, soda lime vs control |
| 6.1.8 | Core | Investigate and describe the effects of varying light intensity, CO₂ concentration and temperature on the rate, e.g. in submerged aquatic plants | S13, S14 | Pondweed method, bubbles vs gas volume, variables, LED, acclimatise, repeat → mean; all three graphs explained (temperature via enzymes, effective collisions, denaturation) |
| 6.1.9 | Supplement | Investigate and describe the effect of light and dark conditions on gas exchange in an aquatic plant using hydrogencarbonate indicator solution | S16, S17 | Indicator colours; light / dark / no-leaf control; dim light; net uptake; respiration myth; the one pause-and-predict |
| 6.1.10 | Supplement | Balanced chemical equation 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ | S05 | Equation read aloud, atom-by-atom balance check |
| 6.1.11 | Supplement | Identify and explain the limiting factors of photosynthesis in different environmental conditions | S15 | Definition; reading the rising vs flat part of a graph; higher-CO₂ curve; dawn / winter / summer |
| 6.2.1 | Core | Most leaves have a large surface area and are thin; explain how these are adaptations for photosynthesis | S09 | Broad → absorb more light; thin → short diffusion distance for CO₂ |
| 6.2.2 | Core | Identify chloroplasts, cuticle, guard cells and stomata, upper and lower epidermis, palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll, air spaces, vascular bundles, xylem and phloem in a dicot leaf | S09, S10 (+ S06 for chloroplasts) | Every structure labelled on one cross-section, then recapped top to bottom |
| 6.2.3 | Supplement | Explain how the structures listed in 6.2.2 adapt leaves for photosynthesis | S09, S10 | One adaptation per structure (transparent cuticle/epidermis, packed palisade with most chloroplasts, air spaces for diffusion, xylem brings water and ions, phloem removes sucrose and amino acids, stomata and guard cells for gas exchange) |

Cross-topic links the chapter relies on (taught briefly, in context): iodine test for starch (Topic 4) in S11;
enzymes, kinetic energy, effective collisions and denaturation (Topic 5) in S14; xylem and phloem roles (Topic 8) in S04 and S10;
respiration happening all the time (Topic 12) in S17.

Exam-technique points included (from mark-scheme style and Save My Exams notes): why destarch; why boil; why ethanol is heated in a
water bath; why a white tile; what a control is and what it shows; why gas volume beats bubble counting; LED / heat;
independent, dependent and control variables; acclimatise and repeat; "describe" vs "explain" a graph; naming a limiting
factor with its reason; "net" uptake; "transfers" not "makes" energy.
