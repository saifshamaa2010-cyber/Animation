| 1 | Almost 400 years ago van Helmont grew a willow in a pot of dried soil for five years, giving it only water; the tree gained about 74 kg while the soil lost about 57 g. | ⚠️ | [CSUN – Where does the mass in plant growth come from?][csun]; [SAPS – van Helmont's experiments][saps]; [Pearson – van Helmont's data][pearson] | Published 1648 (≈ 378 years ago). Gain 164 lb ≈ 74.4 kg; soil loss 2 oz ≈ 57 g. Sources describe dried soil and water (rainwater) only. |
| 2 | Van Helmont concluded the tree was made of water; most of a tree's dry mass (the wood) comes from carbon dioxide; a living tree contains a lot of water. | ✅ | [CSUN][csun]; [Pearson][pearson] | Carbon (≈ half of dry mass) comes from CO₂; the soil's small loss is mineral ions (plus error). |
| 3 | Photosynthesis is the process by which plants synthesise carbohydrates from raw materials using energy from light. | ✅ | [0610 syllabus 2026–28 §6.1][syl] | Syllabus wording. "The carbohydrate it makes is glucose": the syllabus convention (biochemically the first sugars made are three-carbon sugar phosphates, which are built into glucose), so the script avoids saying glucose is made *first*. |
| 4 | Word equation, "in the presence of light and chlorophyll"; light and chlorophyll are conditions, not reactants. | ✅ | [0610 syllabus §6.1][syl]; [Tutopiya – Photosynthesis (0610)][tutopiya-ps] | — |
| 5 | CO₂ diffuses into the leaf from the air (through stomata); water is absorbed by roots and travels in the xylem to the leaf; oxygen is a waste product, some used in respiration, the rest diffuses out. | ✅ | [Save My Exams – Leaf structure (CIE IGCSE)][sme-leaf]; [ZNotes 0610 plant nutrition][znotes] | — |
| 6 | Balanced equation 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂; 6 C, 12 H, 18 O on each side. | ✅ | [0610 syllabus §6.1 Supplement][syl] | Atom counts checked by arithmetic. |
| 7 | Chlorophyll is a green pigment in chloroplasts; it absorbs light and transfers energy from light into energy in chemicals for the synthesis of carbohydrates; energy can't be made. | ✅ | [0610 syllabus §6.1][syl] | "Energy cannot be created or destroyed" = conservation of energy. |
| 8 | Five uses: respiration (release energy), starch (energy store; insoluble, so no effect on osmosis), cellulose (cell walls), sucrose (transport in phloem), nectar (attract insects for pollination). | ✅ | [0610 syllabus 2026–28 §6.1 item 5][ss]; [Tutopiya – Plant nutrition notes][tutopiya-pn] | The 2026–28 list adds nectar to the 2023–25 list. |
| 9 | Glucose contains only C, H, O; amino acids contain nitrogen; nitrate ions are needed to make amino acids (→ proteins); deficiency → stunted growth, older leaves yellow. | ✅ | [0610 syllabus §6.1 item 6][syl]; [Tutopiya – Mineral requirements][tutopiya-min]; [StudyMind – deficiencies][studymind] | Sources differ on the exact yellowing pattern; "older leaves turn yellow" is the exam-standard description. |
| 10 | Magnesium ions are needed to make chlorophyll; deficiency → yellowing between the veins → less light absorbed → less photosynthesis. | ✅ | [Tutopiya – Mineral requirements][tutopiya-min]; [Yara – chlorosis in brassicas][yara] | — |
| 11 | Most leaves are broad and thin: large surface area to absorb light; short diffusion distance for CO₂. | ✅ | [0610 syllabus §6.2.1][scribd]; [Save My Exams – Leaf structure][sme-leaf] | — |
| 12 | Waxy cuticle reduces water loss and is transparent; upper epidermis transparent with no chloroplasts; palisade cells tall, tightly packed, near the top, many chloroplasts, most photosynthesis. | ✅ | [Save My Exams – Leaf structure][sme-leaf]; [ZNotes][znotes] | — |
| 13 | Spongy mesophyll: rounder, loosely packed cells with air spaces that let CO₂ diffuse and give a large surface area for gas exchange; xylem brings water and mineral ions; phloem carries sucrose and amino acids away; stomata in the lower epidermis, opened and closed by guard cells; in the light the guard cells open the stomata so CO₂ can diffuse in. | ✅ | [Save My Exams – Leaf structure][sme-leaf]; [ZNotes][znotes]; [0610 syllabus §6.2][syl]; [Seneca – Stomata (CIE IGCSE)][seneca-stomata]; [TutorChase – guard cells][tc-guard] | Diagram simplified (said aloud). |
| 14 | Destarch: keep the plant in the dark for a day or two so it uses up stored starch; then any starch found was made during the experiment. | ✅ | [Save My Exams – Need for chlorophyll, light and CO₂][sme-need]; [Nuffield – testing leaves for starch][nuffield] | Sources give 24–48 h (some 2–3 days): "a day or two". |
| 15 | Starch test: boiling water ≈ 1 min kills cells and breaks down membranes; Bunsen off, leaf in ethanol in hot water (ethanol is flammable) removes chlorophyll; warm water softens the brittle leaf; white tile; iodine: blue-black = starch, orange-brown = none. | ✅ | [Nuffield][nuffield]; [Save My Exams – Edexcel practical][sme-prac]; [Save My Exams – CIE need for…][sme-need] | Boiling times in sources: 30 s–5 min: "about a minute". |
| 16 | Controls: identical except for the factor being investigated; variegated leaf (only green parts → blue-black; the green parts are the control); black paper (only uncovered part); soda lime absorbs CO₂ (only the control leaf); the leaf in the flask stays attached to the destarched plant. | ✅ | [Save My Exams – need for chlorophyll, light and CO₂][sme-need] | — |
| 17 | Pondweed in water with sodium hydrogencarbonate (supplies CO₂) gives off bubbles of gas, mostly oxygen; gas volume is more accurate than counting bubbles (bubbles vary in size); closer lamp = higher light intensity; independent variable = the one you change, dependent = the one you measure; keep constant: plant, CO₂ concentration, temperature, no other lights; a water bath keeps the temperature steady; LED gives off less heat; wait at each distance; repeat and take a mean; vary CO₂ with different concentrations of sodium hydrogencarbonate; vary temperature with the water bath at different temperatures, changing one factor at a time. | ✅ | [Save My Exams – Edexcel practical: investigating photosynthesis][sme-prac]; [Save My Exams – Investigating the rate of photosynthesis (CIE IGCSE)][sme-rate]; [Save My Exams – Investigating factors affecting the rate (OCR A Level)][sme-ocr]; [Save My Exams – Limiting factors (CIE)][sme-lim] | The CIE IGCSE note varies CO₂ by dissolving different amounts of sodium hydrogencarbonate and varies the water temperature; the water-bath method is from the A Level notes. Example temperatures on screen are settings, not results. |
| 18 | Light and CO₂: rate rises, then levels off; temperature: rises to an optimum then falls quickly, because the reactions are catalysed by enzymes (more kinetic energy → more frequent effective collisions; above the optimum enzymes are denatured, active site changes shape). | ✅ | [Save My Exams – Limiting factors (CIE)][sme-lim]; [0610 syllabus §5 Supplement (effective collisions)][syl] | — |
| 19 | Limiting factor: something in short supply that limits the rate; rising part of a graph → the x-axis factor is limiting; flat part → another factor; raising CO₂ raises the plateau. | ✅ | [Save My Exams – Limiting factors (CIE)][sme-lim]; [Save My Exams – Limiting factors (CIE O Level)][sme-lim-o] | — |
| 20 | Dawn / dull day → light usually limiting (not much light); cold winter's day → temperature often (less kinetic energy, fewer effective collisions); bright warm summer's day → CO₂ often (only a tiny amount in the air). | ⚠️ | [Save My Exams – Limiting factors (CIE)][sme-lim]; [Tutopiya – Photosynthesis][tutopiya-ps]; [Climate.gov – CO₂ ≈ 421 ppm][co2] | Textbook generalisations, hence "usually" and "often". 421 ppm ≈ 0.04 % of the air ("a tiny amount"; no number is said). |
| 21 | Hydrogencarbonate indicator: red at atmospheric CO₂, yellow with more CO₂, purple with less. Tubes sealed with bungs so no CO₂ is exchanged with the air. Pondweed (an aquatic plant, as the syllabus statement says) in light → purple (photosynthesis faster than respiration); in a foil-wrapped tube → yellow (respiration only); no pondweed → stays red (control); dim light → rates equal → no net uptake or release → red. | ✅ | [Wikipedia – Bicarbonate indicator][bic]; [Save My Exams – Investigating gas exchange (CIE)][sme-gas]; [Biology Notes Online – light and dark gas exchange][bno] | Some sources call the starting colour "orange-red". |
| 22 | Plants respire all the time, day and night; in bright light there is a net (overall) uptake of CO₂. | ✅ | [Save My Exams – Investigating gas exchange][sme-gas] | — |
| 23 | A dicotyledonous plant's seeds have two seed leaves (cotyledons). | ✅ | [Britannica – Dicotyledon][brit-dicot] | "Dicot" is an informal grouping, but the two-seed-leaf definition is the standard one. |
